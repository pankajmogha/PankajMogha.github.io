Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics are transforming how we study complex tissues. Single-cell RNA sequencing reveals gene expression differences between individual cells, while spatial transcriptomics preserves information about where those cells or transcripts are located within tissue architecture. Together, these approaches help connect cellular identity, molecular state, neighborhood organization, and tissue function.

This project aligns with my current interest in applying single-cell and spatial approaches to developmental and disease-relevant biological systems. These methods are especially useful for identifying rare cell states, reconstructing differentiation trajectories, comparing healthy and diseased tissue environments, and understanding how local neighborhoods influence cell behavior.

Current single-cell approaches include droplet-based sequencing, plate-based sequencing, single-nucleus RNA sequencing, multiome assays, lineage tracing, and perturbation screens. Spatial transcriptomics methods include array-based capture platforms, imaging-based methods such as MERFISH and seqFISH, and sequencing-based in situ approaches. These techniques can be integrated with histology, immunofluorescence, epigenomics, proteomics, and computational cell-type annotation to build molecular maps of tissues and disease models.

The major strength of single-cell sequencing is its ability to resolve diverse cell states and rare populations, but it usually loses spatial context during tissue dissociation. Dissociation can also introduce stress responses, selectively lose fragile cell types, and alter the apparent composition of a sample. Spatial transcriptomics preserves tissue organization, but current platforms vary widely in resolution, sensitivity, gene coverage, throughput, cost, and tissue compatibility.

This project focuses on using single-cell and spatial transcriptomic approaches to understand how cellular identity, tissue organization, and molecular programs interact in development and disease-relevant biological systems.
