Organ-on-chip systems are engineered microphysiological platforms that recreate key structural, mechanical, biochemical, and cellular features of human tissues in vitro. They are needed because conventional two-dimensional cell culture and many animal models often fail to capture the complex tissue architecture, fluid flow, matrix mechanics, vascular interfaces, immune interactions, and disease-relevant microenvironments that shape human biology.

This project builds on my work with vascularized and innervated in vitro models, including alveolosphere-on-chip systems for pollutant exposure, innervated vasculature-on-chip models for nociception, and microvascular networks shaped by normal and lung cancer-associated fibroblasts. These systems provide controlled ways to study cell-cell communication, vascular function, tissue organization, disease phenotypes, and therapeutic response in human-relevant settings.

Current organ-on-chip technologies combine microfabrication, biomaterials, stem cell biology, primary cell culture, extracellular matrix engineering, live imaging, biosensors, and computational analysis. Common platforms include lung-on-chip, gut-on-chip, liver-on-chip, vascularized tissue chips, tumor-on-chip, and multi-organ systems. These models can incorporate perfusable channels, endothelial barriers, epithelial compartments, hydrogels, mechanical stretch, oxygen gradients, immune cells, and patient-derived cells.

Despite their promise, organ-on-chip models still face important limitations. Device fabrication and operation can be technically demanding, and protocols are not yet standardized across laboratories. Reproducing full tissue complexity remains difficult, especially for long-term maturation, innervation, immune integration, vascular perfusion, and organ-organ communication. Many platforms have low throughput compared with standard screening methods, and biological variability from primary or stem-cell-derived cells can complicate interpretation.

This project focuses on building and applying organ-on-chip systems that better capture human tissue microenvironments for disease modeling, therapeutic discovery, and mechanistic studies.
